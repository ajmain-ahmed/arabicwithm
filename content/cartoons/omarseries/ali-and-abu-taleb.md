## Script

---

### 0:00 — What is this that I saw you both doing?

**Arabic:** مَا هَذَا الَّذِي رَأَيْتُكُمَا تَفْعَلَانِ؟  
**Plain:** ما هذا الذي رأيتكما تفعلان؟

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| مَا | mā | what | A1 |
| هَذَا | hādhā | this | A1 |
| الَّذِي | alladhī | that / which | A2 |
| رَأَيْتُكُمَا | raʾaytukumā | I saw you both | B1 |
| تَفْعَلَانِ | tafʿalāni | you both do | B1 |

**Note:** *الَّذِي* = "that / which" — connects a description to a noun. Think of it as starting a mini-sentence: "the thing **that** I saw."  
**Note:** *رَأَيْتُكُمَا* = verb + pronoun glued together: *رَأَيْتُ* ("I saw") + *ـكُمَا* ("you both").  
**Note:** The ending *ـَانِ* on a verb means "**you both**" (exactly two people — not one, not many).

---

### 0:04 — You were bowing and standing, and it is not the kind of prostration we know with our idols.

**Arabic:** تَسْجُدَانِ وَتَقُومَانِ، وَلَيْسَ السُّجُودَ الَّذِي نَعْرِفُ عِنْدَ أَصْنَامِنَا.  
**Plain:** تسجدان وتقومان، وليس السجود الذي نعرف عند أصنامنا.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| تَسْجُدَانِ | tasjudāni | you both prostrate | B1 |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| تَقُومَانِ | taqūmāni | you both stand | B1 |
| وَلَيْسَ | wa-laysa | and [it] is not | A2 |
| السُّجُودَ | as-sujūda | the prostration | A2 |
| الَّذِي | alladhī | that / which | A2 |
| نَعْرِفُ | naʿrifu | we know | A1 |
| عِنْدَ | ʿinda | at / with | A1 |
| أَصْنَامِنَا | aṣnāminā | our idols | A2 |

**Note:** *وَلَيْسَ* = "and it is not." *لَيْسَ* changes the ending sound of the next word — that's why it's *السُّجُودَ* (ending in *ـَ*) instead of *السُّجُودُ* (ending in *ـُ*).  
**Note:** *أَصْنَامِنَا* = *أَصْنَام* (idols) + *ـنَا* (our) = "our idols." The singular is *صَنَم* (one idol).

---

### 0:10 — And I've never seen you prostrate to our idols before.

**Arabic:** وَمَا رَأَيْتُكُمَا تَسْجُدَانِ لِأَصْنَامِنَا مِنْ قَبْلُ.  
**Plain:** وما رأيتكما تسجدان لأصنامنا من قبل.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| مَا | mā | not | A1 |
| رَأَيْتُكُمَا | raʾaytukumā | I saw you both | B1 |
| تَسْجُدَانِ | tasjudāni | you both prostrate | B1 |
| لِأَصْنَامِنَا | li-aṣnāminā | to our idols | A2 |
| مِنْ | min | from | A1 |
| قَبْلُ | qablu | before | A1 |

**Note:** *مَا* here does **not** mean "what" — it means **"not."** Put it before a past verb to make it negative: *مَا رَأَيْتُ* = "I did not see."  
**Note:** *مِنْ قَبْلُ* = a set phrase meaning "before" or "previously." Use them together.

---

### 0:14 — It is the prayer, father, the prayer that Allah has made obligatory on His Prophet.

**Arabic:** إِنَّهَا الصَّلَاةُ يَا أَبَتِ، الصَّلَاةُ الَّتِي فَرَضَهَا اللَّهُ عَلَى نَبِيِّهِ.  
**Plain:** إنها الصلاة يا أبتِ، الصلاة التي فرضها الله على نبيه.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| إِنَّهَا | innahā | indeed it is / truly it | A2 |
| الصَّلَاةُ | aṣ-ṣalātu | the prayer | A1 |
| يَا | yā | O / oh (when calling someone) | A1 |
| أَبَتِ | abati | my father (dear father) | A2 |
| الصَّلَاةُ | aṣ-ṣalātu | the prayer | A1 |
| الَّتِي | allatī | that / which (feminine) | A2 |
| فَرَضَهَا | faraḍahā | He made it obligatory | A2 |
| اللَّهُ | Allāhu | Allah / God | A1 |
| عَلَى | ʿalā | upon / on | A1 |
| نَبِيِّهِ | nabiyyihi | his prophet | A2 |

**Note:** *إِنَّهَا* = *إِنَّ* ("indeed / truly") + *هَا* ("it"). Like underlining the sentence: "It **REALLY** is the prayer."  
**Note:** *أَبَتِ* = an old, respectful way to say "my father." In modern Arabic, people say *أَبِي*. The *ـتِ* ending is classical.  
**Note:** *الَّتِي* = the feminine form of *الَّذِي*. Use it when describing a feminine noun (here: *الصَّلَاةُ*).  
**Note:** *فَرَضَهَا* = *فَرَضَ* ("He made obligatory") + *ـهَا* ("it" — feminine, pointing back to "the prayer").  
**Note:** *نَبِيِّهِ* = *نَبِي* (prophet) + *ـهِ* ("his"). The "i" sounds link it to the word before (*عَلَى*).

### 0:20 — The Prophet? You mean your nephew Muhammad?

**Arabic:** النَّبِيِّ؟ تَعْنِي ابْنَ أَخِيكَ مُحَمَّد؟  
**Plain:** النبي؟ تعني ابن أخيك محمد؟

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| النَّبِيِّ | an-nabiyyi | the prophet | A1 |
| تَعْنِي | taʿnī | you mean / you intend | A2 |
| ابْنَ | ibna | son | A1 |
| أَخِيكَ | akhīka | your brother | A2 |
| مُحَمَّد | muḥammad | Muhammad | A1 |

**Note:** *ابْنَ* here has an *ـَ* ending because it's the target of the question ("you mean **whom?**"). It's like saying "Do you mean **him**?"  
**Note:** *أَخِيكَ* = *أَخ* (brother) becomes *أَخِي* with "your" added: *أَخِي* + *ـكَ* = *أَخِيكَ*. The long "ee" sound in the middle is part of the pattern.

### 0:26 — Yes, by Allah — he is truly a Prophet, and revelation comes down to him with the words of Allah... the Qur'an.

**Arabic:** بَلَى وَاللَّهِ، إِنَّهُ لَنَبِيٌّ، وَإِنَّ الْوَحْيَ لَيَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِ بِكَلَامِ اللَّهِ.. الْقُرْآن.  
**Plain:** بلى والله، إنه لنبي، وإن الوحي ليتنزل عليه بكلام الله.. القرآن.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| بَلَى | balā | yes (strong correction) | A2 |
| وَاللَّهِ | wa-llāhi | by Allah / I swear by God | A1 |
| إِنَّهُ | innahu | indeed he is / truly he | A2 |
| لَنَبِيٌّ | la-nabiyyun | truly a prophet | B1 |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| إِنَّ | inna | indeed / verily | A2 |
| الْوَحْيَ | al-waḥya | the revelation | B1 |
| لَيَتَنَزَّلُ | la-yatanazzalu | indeed it descends / comes down | B1 |
| عَلَيْهِ | ʿalayhi | upon him | A1 |
| بِكَلَامِ | bi-kalāmi | with the words / speech of | A2 |
| اللَّهِ | Allāhi | Allah (linked form) | A1 |
| الْقُرْآن | al-qurʾān | the Qur'an | A1 |

**Note:** *بَلَى* = "yes" used to strongly correct someone or answer a doubting question. Much stronger than *نَعَم*.  
**Note:** *وَاللَّهِ* = "By Allah!" — an oath. When Arabs use Allah's name in a promise, the name gets an "i" ending: *Allā-hi*.  
**Note:** *إِنَّهُ* = *إِنَّ* ("indeed") + *هُ* ("he"). Same pattern as *إِنَّهَ* but for masculine.  
**Note:** *لَنَبِيٌّ* — the *لَـ* at the front adds super emphasis, like writing the word in **ALL CAPS**. "He **IS** a prophet — no doubt."  
**Note:** *إِنَّ* grabs attention: "Listen! The revelation…" The important word after it (*الْوَحْيَ*) gets an "a" ending.  
**Note:** *لَيَتَنَزَّلُ* — the *لَـ* adds the same strong emphasis: "it **DOES** come down." The *تَنـ* in the middle means the action happens bit by bit — perfect for describing revelation coming slowly.  
**Note:** *بِكَلَامِ* + *اللَّهِ* = "with the speech of Allah." The "i" ending on both words links them together into a chain: "the speech **of** Allah."

### 0:34 — And I follow his religion — Islam.

**Arabic:** وَأَنَا عَلَى دِينِهِ، الْإِسْلَام.  
**Plain:** وأنا على دينه، الإسلام.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| أَنَا | anā | I | A1 |
| عَلَى | ʿalā | on / upon | A1 |
| دِينِهِ | dīnihi | his religion | A1 |
| الْإِسْلَام | al-islām | Islam | A1 |

**Note:** *دِينِهِ* = *دِين* (religion) + *ـهِ* ("his"). No separate word for "his" — it sticks directly to the end.  
**Note:** *عَلَى دِينِهِ* = literally "on his religion." In Arabic, you say you're "on" someone's religion to mean you follow it.

### 0:46 — You've said a lot, my son, sit down and tell me.

**Arabic:** قَدْ أَكْثَرْتَ عَلَيَّ يَا وَلَدِي، اجْلِسْ وَحَدِّثْنِي.  
**Plain:** قد أكثرت علي يا ولدي، اجلس وحدثني.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| قَدْ | qad | indeed / (emphasis) | B1 |
| أَكْثَرْتَ | aktharta | you did/said much | A2 |
| عَلَيَّ | ʿalayya | upon me | A2 |
| يَا | yā | O / oh | A1 |
| وَلَدِي | waladī | my son | A1 |
| اجْلِسْ | ijlis | sit! | A1 |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| حَدِّثْنِي | ḥaddithnī | tell me | A2 |

**Note:** *قَدْ* adds emphasis before a past verb. Like the difference between "you said" and "you **HAVE** said."  
**Note:** *أَكْثَرْتَ عَلَيَّ* = literally "you did much upon me" — a gentle idiom meaning "you've talked a lot."  
**Note:** *عَلَيَّ* = "upon me." When you attach "me" to this word, the ending changes to *ـَيَّ*.  
**Note:** *وَلَدِي* = *وَلَد* (son) + *ـي* (my) = "my son."  
**Note:** *حَدِّثْنِي* = *حَدِّثْ* ("tell!") + *ـنِي* ("me") = "tell me!" The "me" sticks directly to the end of the verb.

### 0:53 — What is this religion you speak of, and what does it call to?

**Arabic:** مَا هَذَا الدِّينُ الَّذِي تَذْكُرُ؟ وَإِلَى مَا يَدْعُو؟  
**Plain:** ما هذا الدين الذي تذكر؟ وإلى ما يدعو؟

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| مَا | mā | what | A1 |
| هَذَا | hādhā | this | A1 |
| الدِّينُ | ad-dīnu | the religion | A1 |
| الَّذِي | alladhī | that / which | A2 |
| تَذْكُرُ | tadhkuru | you mention / speak of | A1 |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| إِلَى | ilā | to | A1 |
| مَا | mā | what | A1 |
| يَدْعُو | yadʿū | it calls / he calls | A1 |

**Note:** *الدِّينُ* — when *الـ* ("the") comes before this word, the "l" drops and the "d" is doubled: **ad-dīn**. This happens with certain Arabic letters.

## Grammar Points

| # | Pattern | How It Works | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **ـَانِ verb ending** | The ending *ـَانِ* means "**you both**" (exactly two people). Used for addressing two people. | تَسْجُدَانِ = you both prostrate / تَقُومَانِ = you both stand |
| 2 | **ـكُمَا suffix** | "you both" — attached to the end of a verb. The verb and pronoun become one word. | رَأَيْتُكُمَا = I saw you both |
| 3 | **لَيْسَ + noun** | "It is not." *لَيْسَ* changes the next word's ending to *ـَ* (instead of *ـُ*). | وَلَيْسَ السُّجُودَ الَّذِي نَعْرِفُ = it is not the prostration we know |
| 4 | **الَّذِي / الَّتِي** | "that / which" — connects a description to a noun. *الَّذِي* for masculine nouns, *الَّتِي* for feminine nouns. | الصَّلَاةُ الَّتِي فَرَضَهَا اللَّهُ = the prayer that Allah made obligatory |
| 5 | **إِنَّ + pronoun** | "Indeed / truly." *إِنَّ* adds emphasis and must cling to a pronoun after it. Changes the next word's ending. | إِنَّهَا الصَّلَاةُ = indeed it is the prayer / إِنَّهُ لَنَبِيٌّ = indeed he is a prophet |
| 6 | **لَـ emphasis** | The particle *لَـ* in front of a noun adds super emphasis, like bold or ALL CAPS. | لَنَبِيٌّ = truly a prophet / لَيَتَنَزَّلُ = it indeed descends |
| 7 | **verb + pronoun suffix** | "Me / you / him" sticks directly to the end of verbs. No separate word needed. | فَرَضَهَا = He made it obligatory / حَدِّثْنِي = tell me |
| 8 | **نoun + pronoun suffix** | "My / your / his / our" sticks directly to the end of nouns. | أَصْنَامِنَا = our idols / دِينِهِ = his religion / وَلَدِي = my son |
| 9 | **قَدْ + past verb** | Adds emphasis to a past tense verb. Like "you **HAVE** said" vs. "you said." | قَدْ أَكْثَرْتَ = you have indeed said much |
| 10 | **Imperative verb** | Command form — address someone directly. Drop the subject, just say the action. | اجْلِسْ = sit! / حَدِّثْنِي = tell me! |
| 11 | **بِـ + noun chain** | "With / by." When *بِـ* starts a chain, the noun gets an *ـِ* ending to link to the next word. | بِكَلَامِ اللَّهِ = with the words of Allah |
