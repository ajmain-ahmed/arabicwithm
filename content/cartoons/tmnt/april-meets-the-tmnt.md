### 0:00 — Will you be okay?

**Arabic:** هَلْ سَتَكُونِينَ بِخَيْر؟  
**Plain:** هل ستكونين بخير؟

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| هَلْ | hal | will...? / do...? | A1 |
| سَتَكُونِينَ | satakūnīna | you (f.) will be | A2 |
| بِخَيْر | bi-khayr | fine / well | A1 |

**Note:** *هَلْ* goes at the start of a sentence to turn it into a yes/no question.  
**Note:** The *ـينَ* ending on *سَتَكُونِينَ* means "you" when talking to **one female**. For a male, the ending would be different.

### 0:03 — My aunt is always happy for me to stay here.

**Arabic:** خَالَتِي تُرَحِّبُ بِبَقَائِي هُنَا دَائِمًا.  
**Plain:** خالتي ترحب ببقائي هنا دائما.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| خَالَتِي | khālatī | my aunt (mother's side) | A2 |
| تُرَحِّبُ | turaḥḥibu | she welcomes / is happy to have | A2 |
| بِبَقَائِي | bi-baqāʾī | for me to stay | B1 |
| هُنَا | hunā | here | A1 |
| دَائِمًا | dāʾiman | always | A1 |

**Note:** *خَالَة* = aunt on your **mother's side**. The *ـي* at the end = "my." Literally: "aunt-my."  
**Note:** *بِبَقَائِي* packs a whole idea into one word: *بَقَاء* (staying) + *ـي* (my) + *بِ* (with) = "with my staying."

### 0:06 — But I won't rest until I find the ones who kidnapped my dad.

**Arabic:** وَلَكِنْ لَنْ أَرْتَاحَ قَبْلَ أَنْ أَعْثُرَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ اخْتَطَفُوا أَبِي.  
**Plain:** ولكن لن أرتاح قبل أن أعثر على الذين اختطفوا أبي.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| وَلَكِنْ | wa-lakin | but | A1 |
| لَنْ | lan | will not | A2 |
| أَرْتَاحَ | artāḥa | I rest / I relax | A2 |
| قَبْلَ | qabla | before | A1 |
| أَنْ | an | to | A2 |
| أَعْثُرَ | aʿthura | I find | A2 |
| عَلَى | ʿalā | on | A1 |
| الَّذِينَ | alladhīna | the ones who / those who | A2 |
| اخْتَطَفُوا | ikhtaṭafū | they kidnapped | B1 |
| أَبِي | abī | my father / my dad | A1 |

**Note:** *لَنْ* + verb = **"will not ___"** — a strong future negation. Just put *لَنْ* before any present verb.  
**Note:** *أَنْ* connects two verbs: "before *to* find" = "before (I) find." Use it after words like "before," "after," or "want."  
**Note:** After the verb *"find"* (أَعْثُرَ), Arabic always adds *عَلَى* ("on"). So "find them" is literally "find on them."  
**Note:** *الَّذِينَ* = "the ones who" — used for **groups of men or mixed groups**. For women only, use *اللاَّتِي*.  
**Note:** The ending *ـوا* on *اخْتَطَفُوا* means **"they"** (a group of males).  
**Note:** *أَب* (father) + "my" changes to *أَبِي* ("my father"). A small exception — just remember it.

### 0:10 — Won't the police help you?

**Arabic:** أَلَنْ تُسَاعِدَكِ الشُّرْطَةُ؟

**Note:** *أَلَنْ* = a question form of *لَنْ* ("will not"). The *ـكِ* ending means "you" when talking to **a female**. For a male, it would be *ـكَ*.

### 0:13 — When I told them that alien robots kidnapped my dad...

**Arabic:** عِنْدَمَا أَخْبَرْتُهُمْ أَنَّ الْعُقُولَ الْآلِيَّةَ الْفَضَائِيَّةَ اخْتَطَفَتْ أَبِي،  
**Plain:** عندما أخبرتهم أن العقول الآلية الفضائية اختطفت أبي،

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| عِنْدَمَا | ʿindamā | when | A2 |
| أَخْبَرْتُهُمْ | ʾakhbaratuhum | I told them | A2 |
| أَنَّ | ʾanna | that | A2 |
| الْعُقُولَ | al-ʿuqūla | the brains / the minds | B1 |
| الْآلِيَّةَ | al-ʾāliyya | the mechanical / robotic | B1 |
| الْفَضَائِيَّةَ | al-faḍāʾiyya | the alien / from space | B1 |
| اخْتَطَفَتْ | ikhtaṭafat | she/it kidnapped | B1 |
| أَبِي | abī | my father / my dad | A1 |

**Note:** *أَنَّ* ("that") changes the ending of the next word to *ـَ* instead of *ـُ*. That's why it's *الْعُقُولَ* not *الْعُقُولُ*.  
**Note:** *اخْتَطَفَتْ* looks like "she kidnapped," but it means **"they kidnapped"** — because in Arabic, groups of **non-human things** (robots, cars, books) use feminine singular verbs.  
**Note:** The three words *الْعُقُولَ الْآلِيَّةَ الْفَضَائِيَّةَ* stack together: "the minds the mechanical the space" = "the alien robot minds." In Arabic, adjectives come **after** the noun and also carry *الـ*.

### 0:17 — ...they called me a liar and didn't believe me.

**Arabic:** كَذَّبُونِي وَلَمْ يُصَدِّقُوا مَا قُلْتُهُ.  
**Plain:** كذبوني ولم يصدقوا ما قلته.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| كَذَّبُونِي | kaḏḏabūnī | they called me a liar | B1 |
| وَ | wa | and | A1 |
| لَمْ | lam | did not | A2 |
| يُصَدِّقُوا | yuṣaddiqū | they believe | A2 |
| مَا | mā | what / the thing that | A1 |
| قُلْتُهُ | qultuhu | I said it | A2 |

**Note:** *كَذَّبُونِي* does **not** mean "they lied" — it means **"they called me a liar."** The *ـني* ending = "me."  
**Note:** *لَمْ* removes the final *ـنَ* sound from a "they" verb. Normally "they believe" is *يُصَدِّقُونَ*; after *لَمْ* it becomes *يُصَدِّقُوا*.  
**Note:** *قُلْتُهُ* = "I said **it**" — Arabic adds an extra "it" (*ـهُ*) even when English doesn't. "What I said" becomes "what I said **it**."

### 0:21 — April, I promise we won't stop until we find him.

**Arabic:** أَبْرِيل، أَعِدُكِ أَنَّنَا لَنْ نَهْدَأَ قَبْلَ أَنْ نَعْثُرَ عَلَيْهِ.  
**Plain:** أبريل، أعدك أننا لن نهدأ قبل أن نعثر عليه.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| أَبْرِيل | abrīl | April (name) | A1 |
| أَعِدُكِ | aʿiduki | I promise you (f.) | A2 |
| أَنَّنَا | annanā | that we | A2 |
| لَنْ | lan | will not | A2 |
| نَهْدَأَ | nahdaʾa | we calm down / we stop | B1 |
| قَبْلَ | qabla | before | A1 |
| أَنْ | an | to | A2 |
| نَعْثُرَ | naʿthura | we find | A2 |
| عَلَيْهِ | ʿalayhi | on him | A1 |

**Note:** *أَعِدُكِ* = "I promise **you**" — the *ـكِ* ending = "you" (feminine, talking to April). For a male: *أَعِدُكَ*.  
**Note:** *أَنَّ* ("that") must always cling to a word after it. Here it clings to *نَا* ("we") → *أَنَّنَا* ("that we").  
**Note:** After *لَنْ*, the verb ending changes from *ـُ* to *ـَ*. That's why it's *نَهْدَأَ* and *نَعْثُرَ*, not *نَهْدَأُ* and *نَعْثُرُ*.  
**Note:** The verb "find" always needs *عَلَى* after it. "Find him" = "find **on** him" (*نَعْثُرَ عَلَيْهِ*).

### 0:24 — We won't. / We will.

**Arabic:** لَنْ نَفْعَلَ. / سَنَفْعَلُ.  
**Plain:** لن نفعل. / سنفعل.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| لَنْ نَفْعَلَ | lan nafʿala | we won't do | A2 |
| سَنَفْعَلُ | sanafʿalu | we will do | A1 |

**Note:** *لَنْ* makes the verb end in *ـَ*; *سَـ* makes it future. Two ways to talk about the future — one negative, one positive.  
**Note:** *نَفْعَلُ* = "we do." The *نَـ* at the start of a verb = "we" (built into the verb).

### 0:28 — This isn't your problem.

**Arabic:** قَضِيَّتُكُمْ لَيْسَتْ قَضِيَّتِي.  
**Plain:** قضيتكم ليست قضيتي.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| قَضِيَّتُكُمْ | qaḍiyyatukum | your problem (pl.) | A1 |
| لَيْسَتْ | laysat | is not (for feminine) | A1 |
| قَضِيَّتِي | qaḍiyyatī | my problem | A1 |

**Note:** *ـكُمْ* = "your" when talking to **a group** (2+ people). One man = *ـكَ*, one woman = *ـكِ*.  
**Note:** Use *لَيْسَتْ* (not *لَيْسَ*) when describing something **feminine**. *قَضِيَّة* is feminine, so it needs *لَيْسَتْ*.

---

### 0:30 — No, it is our problem.

**Arabic:** بَلَى — إِنَّهَا قَضِيَّتُنَا.  
**Plain:** بلى — إنها قضيتنا.

| Arabic | Transliteration | English | CEFR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| بَلَى | balā | yes (after a negative) | A2 |
| إِنَّهَا | innahā | indeed it is / it really is | A2 |
| قَضِيَّتُنَا | qaḍiyyatunā | our problem | A1 |

**Note:** When someone says something **negative** ("This isn't your problem") and you want to say **"Yes, it is"** — use *بَلَى*. Never *نَعَم*. *نَعَم* is only for answering normal questions.  
**Note:** *إِنَّ* adds emphasis, like saying **"indeed"** or **"it really is."** It also clings to the next word — here *هَا* ("it/she") → *إِنَّهَا*.  
**Note:** *ـنَا* at the end = "our." *قَضِيَّة* + *ـنَا* = "our problem."

## Grammar Points

| # | Pattern | How It Works | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **سَـ + verb** | Put *سَ* before a present verb = future ("will ___") | سَتَكُونِينَ = you will be / سَنَفْعَلُ = we will do |
| 2 | **لَنْ + verb** | Put *لَنْ* before a present verb = negative future ("will not ___"). The verb ending changes to *ـَ*. | لَنْ أَرْتَاحَ = I won't rest / لَنْ نَهْدَأَ = we won't stop |
| 3 | **لَمْ + verb** | Put *لَمْ* before a present verb = past negative ("did not ___"). Removes the final *ـنَ*. | لَمْ يُصَدِّقُوا = they didn't believe |
| 4 | **أَنْ / أَنَّ + verb/noun** | *أَنْ* = "to" (connects verbs). *أَنَّ* = "that" (connects a sentence). Both change the next word's ending to *ـَ*. | قَبْلَ أَنْ أَعْثُرَ = before I find / أَنَّنَا لَنْ نَهْدَأَ = that we won't stop |
| 5 | **verb + عَلَى** | The verb "find" always needs *عَلَى* ("on") after it. | أَعْثُرَ عَلَى = find / نَعْثُرَ عَلَيْهِ = we find him |
| 6 | **noun + pronoun suffix** | Add *ـي* (my), *ـكِ* (your f.), *ـكُمْ* (your pl.), *ـنَا* (our) to the end of a noun. | خَالَتِي = my aunt / قَضِيَّتُنَا = our problem |
| 7 | **بَلَى vs نَعَم** | Use *بَلَى* to say "yes" after someone says something negative. Use *نَعَم* for normal yes/no questions. | — "This isn't your problem." — "بَلَى!" (Yes, it is!) |
| 8 | **Adjective chain** | Multiple adjectives stack after the noun, each with *الـ*. | الْعُقُولَ الْآلِيَّةَ الْفَضَائِيَّةَ = the alien robot minds |
| 9 | **Non-human plural = feminine singular** | Groups of non-human things use feminine singular verbs. | اخْتَطَفَتْ العقول = the robots kidnapped (lit. "she kidnapped") |
