## Grammar Points

| Grammar Point | Arabic Name | Simple Explanation | Example from Article |
|---------------|-------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Nominal Sentence | الْجُمْلَةُ الاِسْمِيَّةُ | A sentence that **starts with a noun**. It has two parts: the thing we talk about (subject) + the information (predicate). | هَيْلْدا بَاسِي طَبَّاخَةٌ نَيْجِيرِيَّةٌ.  <br>*(Hilda Baci = subject; Nigerian chef = predicate)* |
| Verbal Sentence | الْجُمْلَةُ الْفِعْلِيَّةُ | A sentence that **starts with a verb**. The verb comes first, then the person who does it. | هِيَ **طَبَخَتْ** أَكْبَرَ قِدْرِ...  <br>*(Verb طَبَخَتْ comes first; هِيَ tells us who)* |
| The word "was" | كَانَ | **كَانَ** means "was." When we use كَانَ, the describing word after it gets an **ـًـ (an)** sound at the end. | الْقِدْرُ كَانَ **كَبِيرًا** جِدًّا.  <br>*(كَبِير becomes كَبِيرًا because of كَانَ)* |
| The Possessive Chain | الإِضَافَةُ | When two nouns are joined together, the second noun explains **what kind** the first noun is. Read it like: "Noun 1 of Noun 2." | أَكْبَرَ **قِدْرِ رُزٍّ**  <br>*("pot of rice" — قِدْرِ tells us what, رُزٍّ tells us what kind)* |
| Pronoun Suffixes | ضَمَائِرُ الْوَصْلِ | Small words like **هَا** (her) can stick to the end of a noun or verb. They save space! | أُمُّ**هَا** = her mother  <br>سَاعَدَتْ**هَا** = helped her |

## Source

> **BBC News, CNN, Al Jazeera — September 2025**
>
> Nigerian celebrity chef Hilda Baci broke the Guinness World Record for the largest-ever pot of jollof rice on September 12, 2025, in Victoria Island, Lagos, Nigeria. She cooked 8,780 kg of jollof rice in a giant pot nearly 20 feet across, using 200 bags of rice over nine hours. The giant pot collapsed under a crane during weighing, but the food survived. Thousands of onlookers were fed with zero food wasted. Guinness World Records confirmed the record on September 15, 2025.
>
> **Quotes:**
> - "It took nine hours of fire, passion, and teamwork" — Hilda Baci
> - "We honestly just wanted to create an experience, wanted to bring people together, you know, through food" — Hilda Baci
