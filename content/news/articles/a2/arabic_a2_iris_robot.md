## 📐 Grammar Points

| # | Grammar Point | Arabic Example | English Explanation | A2 Level |
|---|--------------|----------------|---------------------|----------|
| 1 | **Nominal Sentence (جملة اسمية)** | هُنَاكَ مُشْكِلَةُ نَقْصٍ | "There is a problem of shortage" — uses هُنَاكَ to start a sentence about existence | A2 |
| 2 | **Reported Speech (مَنْقُولٌ)** | قَالَتِ الْوَزِيرَةُ: "سَيُلْهِمُ..." | "The minister said: 'It will inspire...'" — using quotes with قَالَ/قَالَتْ | A2 |
| 3 | **أَنَّ + Verb (Subordinate Clause)** | أَضَافَتْ أَنَّ الْمَرْأَةَ تَقُودُ... | "She added that women lead..." — أَنَّ introduces a full clause with its own verb | A2 |

## 💡 Learning Notes

### Sentence Structure Tips
- This article uses **reported speech** extensively (قَالَتْ...). Notice how Arabic uses quotation marks the same way as English.
- The connector **لِذَا** (therefore) is a powerful A2 word that shows cause and effect — very common in news writing.
- **هَذَا/هَذِهِ** (this m./f.) are demonstrative pronouns that must match the gender of the noun they describe.

### New A2 Patterns Introduced
- **أَنَّ + sentence**: This introduces a subordinate clause after verbs like قَالَتْ (she said) and أَضَافَتْ (she added).
- **لَيْسَ لَهُ/لَهَا** (it has no / she has no): A common pattern for expressing negation of possession.
