## Grammar Points

| Grammar Point | Arabic Example | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **Superlative (أفعل)** | أَحَرِّ السَّنَوَاتِ | The pattern أَفْعَل denotes the superlative: "the hottest of the years." For triliteral roots with a doubled final radical (like ح-ر-ر), the form becomes أَحَرُّ. In genitive construction (إضافة), it becomes أَحَرِّ. |
| **أنَّ + noun clause** | أَنَّ 2025 كَانَتْ... | أنَّ introduces a nominal clause. It always makes the subject accusative. Here "2025" is the subject of the clause. Contrast with إِنَّ which is similar but used in different contexts. |
| **Connector (لذلك)** | لِذَلِكَ، يَجِبُ أَنْ نَتَصَرَّفَ. | لِذَلِكَ means "therefore." It is a result connector used to show consequence. It always begins the second sentence, separated by a comma. |
| **Verbal noun (المصدر)** | إِيجَادُ حَلٍّ | The verbal noun (مصدر) functions like a gerund in English: "finding a solution." It can act as subject, object, or in إضافة. |
| **Definite adjective agreement** | الْبِيئَةِ الْعَالَمِيَّةِ | When a noun is definite (with الـ), its describing adjective must also carry الـ: "the environment the global" = "the global environment." |
| **Numbers with nouns** | 770 مِلْيُونَ إِنْسَانٍ | After a number + million, the counted noun remains singular and in the genitive case: "770 million of humans." |
| **Because (لأن)** | الْأَرْضُ تَحْتَرِقُ لِأَنَّ... | لِأَنَّ means "because." It introduces a reason clause. When followed by a definite noun (الْإِنْسَانَ), that noun becomes accusative (the subject of the أنَّ clause). |
| **After (بعد)** | بَعْدَ ١١ سَنَةً... | بَعْدَ is a time preposition meaning "after." It requires the accusative case on the following noun. Here سَنَةً is in the accusative case. |
| **Because of (بسبب)** | بِسَبَبِ التَّغَيُّرِ | The preposition بِسَبَبِ (because of) requires the genitive case on the following noun. It is a common A2 connector. |
| **But / However (لكن)** | لَكِنَّ التَّغَيُّرَ الْمُنَاخِيَّ... | لكن is a contrasting conjunction meaning "but." When used with إِنَّ (as لَكِنَّ), the following noun becomes accusative. Here التَّغَيُّرَ is in the accusative case. |
