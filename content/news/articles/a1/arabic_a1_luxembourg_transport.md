| هِيَ أَوَّلُ دَوْلَةٍ فِي العَالَمِ تَفْعَلُ ذَلِكَ. | هي أول دولة في العالم تفعل ذلك. | It is the first country in the world to do that. |

**Grammar Note:** أَصْبَحَ (became) is a verb used to describe a change of state. The word after it takes the accusative case (ّاً / -an ending), so مَجَّانِيًّا has two dammas and an alif tanween.

| المَطَارُ هُوَ بِنَاءٌ كَبِيرٌ جِدًّا. | المطار هو بناء كبير جدا. | The airport is a very big building. |
| المَحَطَّةُ قَرِيبَةٌ جِدًّا مِنَ المَطَارِ. | المحطة قريبة جدا من المطار. | The station is very near the airport. |
| قَبْلَ ذَلِكَ، كَانَ المَطَارُ بَعِيدًا. | قبل ذلك، كان المطار بعيدا. | Before that, the airport was far. |

**Grammar Note:** اِنْفَتَحَ (opened / was opened) is a Form VII verb — it starts with اِنْـ and means "it opened" by itself (passive meaning). This form is often used for things that open, start, or begin on their own.

## Paragraph 3: Big Numbers, Fast Trains

| Arabic (with tashkeel) | Plain Arabic | English |

| هُنَاكَ جِسْرٌ جَمِيلٌ فَوْقَ الطَّرِيقِ. | هناك جسر جميل فوق الطريق. | There is a beautiful bridge above the highway. |
| طُولُ الجِسْرِ مِئَةٌ وَعَشْرَةُ أَمْتَارٍ. | طول الجسر مئة وعشرة أمتار. | The bridge's length is one hundred and ten meters. |
| يَنْتَظِرُ النَّاسُ التَّرَامَ كُلَّ ثَمَانِي دَقَائِقَ. | ينتظر الناس الترام كل ثماني دقائق. | People wait for the tram every eight minutes. |

**Grammar Note:** يَمْشِي (it goes / it walks) — the verb مَشَى has two forms: يَمْشِي for "he/it walks/goes" and يَمْشِي for vehicles meaning "it runs/operates." Here it means the tram runs at 70 km/h.

## Grammar Points

| Grammar Point | Explanation | Example from Article |
|---|---|---|
| **ال + noun = "the"** | Add الـ to the beginning of a noun to make it definite ("the"). Sun letters: لـ disappears. | النَّقْلُ = the transport; التَّرَامُ = the tram |
| **Verb forms: يَـ prefix** | Add يَـ at the start of present tense verbs for "he/it." | يَصِلُ = he arrives; يَمْشِي = it goes; يَنْتَظِرُ = he waits |
| **Numbers 1 & 2 agreement** | After number 1 (وَاحِد) and number 2 (اِثْنَان), the noun is singular. | — |
| **Numbers 3-10: plural noun** | Numbers 3-10 use the plural form of the noun with genitive case. | أَرْبَعٌ وَعِشْرُونَ مَحَطَّةً = 24 stations |
| **Adjective after noun** | In Arabic, the adjective comes *after* the noun it describes. | دَوْلَةٌ صَغِيرَةٌ = a small country; التَّرَامُ الجَدِيدُ = the new tram |
| **Accusative case (ـًـ)** | Some words end in ـًـ (two dammas + alif) when they are the object or after certain verbs like أَصْبَحَ. | مَجَّانِيًّا = free (accusative) |
| **Preposition: فَوْقَ = above/over** | فَوْقَ is a preposition meaning "above" or "over." | فَوْقَ الطَّرِيقِ = above the road |
| **Verb: اِنْفَتَحَ (Form VII)** | Add اِنْـ before the root to make a passive meaning: "it opened." | اِنْفَتَحَ التَّرَامُ = the tram opened |
| **Numbers 11-99** | Say the ones digit first, then وَ, then the tens. | سِتَّةٌ وَثَلَاثُونَ = 36 (six and thirty) |
| **Every + number** | Use كُلَّ + number (feminine form) to say "every X." | كُلَّ ثَمَانِي دَقَائِقَ = every eight minutes |
