## 📝 Grammar Points

| # | Grammar Point | Explanation | Example from Article |
|---|--------------|-------------|---------------------|
| 1 | ** Feminine verb ending: تْ** | When the subject is feminine (like مَدِينَةٌ "city"), past-tense verbs get a تْ at the end. Think of it like "she." | أَعْلَنَتْ مَدِينَةُ نِيُويُورْكَ (New York City **she** announced) |
| 2 | **لَيْسَ لَدَى + pronoun = "does not have"** | لَدَى means "at the possession of." Add هَا (her/their) and you get "at their possession." Add لَيْسَ (not) and you get "they don't have." | لَيْسَ لَدَيْهَا إِنْتَرْنِت (at-their-possession not = internet) = "They do not have internet" |
| 3 | **Plural verb ending: ونَ** | The ending ونَ on a present-tense verb means "they" (a group). It turns "he learns" into "they learn." | يَتَعَلَّمُونَ (they learn), يَسْتَخْدِمُونَ (they use) |
| 4 | **Accusative case: ًا (an/tanween)** | When a noun is the object of a verb, it gets two short "a" sounds (ًا) at the end. This is called "accusative case." | خَبَرًا جَدِيدًا (new news — object of "announced"), كَلِمَةً مُهِمَّةً (important sentence — object of "said") |
| 5 | **إِنَّ + هُ = "indeed it is"** | إِنَّ is a strong way to say "indeed." Add هُ (it/he) and you get a forceful "it is." Great for emphasizing! | إِنَّهُ خِدْمَةٌ أَسَاسِيَّةٌ (Indeed-it [is] service essential = "It IS an essential service.") |
